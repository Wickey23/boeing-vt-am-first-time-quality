# M04–M06 bounded physical-layer integration experiment

## Current scope
The sampled STL raster is compared with independent directed triangle-plane section areas. The final partial layer uses its actual midpoint. M05 registers original CSV bytes to those physical layers, checks chronology, Z and traversed XY cells, and reports blocked/review-required states. M06 is an **offline integration experiment only**; no UI or production solver calls it.

## Predeclared numerical gates
- One closed, consistently oriented shell; verified original STL SHA-256 and source CSV SHA-256.
- 2–40 uniform full-height layers, 2–1,500 represented cells; no fractional cells are rounded to full density.
- Registration must have no blockers, including missing powered layers, ambiguous/empty paths and discontinuous chronology.
- Absolute relative sampled/reference section-area discrepancy at most 1% in every layer; sampled/mesh volume discrepancy at most 1%. These are chosen research screening criteria, not process tolerances or proven error bounds.
- Explicit research acknowledgment and supplied bounded material/thermal parameters.
- Existing finite-volume solver must pass its numerical energy balance and stability checks.

## Synthetic verification
Use the public 10 mm cube scaled to 1 mm, two 0.5 mm layers, four cells per layer, and three explicit CSV commands. Expected commanded energy 0.002 J, absorbed energy 0.001 J at 0.5 coupling, eight cells, relative energy imbalance below 1e-9. Reject wrong hash, wrong Z, missing acknowledgment and partial-height layers. Test execution remains subject to exact-head CI.

## Remaining acceptance
M04 still requires quantitative thin-feature, sleeve and rotated bracket accuracy and convergence; neither four-ray occupancy nor directed section area proves local fidelity. M05 still requires source-backed machine-to-model transforms and independent registration validation. M06 requires support for fractional geometry and appropriate physical boundary conditions, worker integration, progress/temperature visualization and benchmarked mesh/time convergence. M07 requires independent measured thermal traces and calibration/held-out validation. No melt pool, porosity, crack, residual stress or distortion prediction is established.

## M07 time-discretization diagnostic
Use fixed step scales 1, 0.5 and 0.25 of the existing stable/motion-limited step ceiling, with identical source bytes, material inputs and spatial cells. Record actual integration step counts, energy balance, final cell-temperature RMSE and maximum difference, peak-temperature differences, command-end maximum-temperature differences, and changes in heuristic consolidation state. The test requires strictly increasing step counts and energy conservation at all three scales; it does not assume monotonic errors or a theoretical convergence order because the cell-state update is discontinuous. No temperature-accuracy acceptance limit is assigned without a documented reference problem or independent experimental data. Spatial convergence and model validation remain separate gates.

## M07 matched XY refinement
Use 0.5, 0.25 and 0.125 mm XY pitches on the same 1 mm cube, with unchanged 0.5 mm physical layer height, scan coordinates, properties and 10 microsecond common time-step ceiling. Expect 8, 32 and 128 full sampled cells. Require identical realized integration step counts for every command. Average four nested fine cells into each coarse cell before calculating RMSE and maximum differences; reject changes in occupied material or origin. Preserve commanded and absorbed energy at each resolution. This tests XY sensitivity only, not Z convergence or experimental accuracy; no convergence acceptance tolerance is yet established.

## M07 analytical semi-discrete benchmark
Two 0.5 mm cells, initially at 25 C, with a fixed 100 C bottom plate and insulated other faces. Constant conductivity 2 W/(m K), density 7800 kg/m3, heat capacity 500 J/(kg K); zero beam power. Activate the lower cell at t=0 and upper cell at t=0.1 s; stop at 0.3 s. Let r=k/(rho cp dz^2), u=T-Tplate. Before upper activation u'=-2ru; after activation the two-cell system is u'=r[[-3,1],[1,-1]]u, with exact matrix exponential. Reference values: first command 50.2389921 C; final lower 69.4853144 C; final upper 37.4530441 C. Predeclared test: 1, 0.5, 0.25 ms time-step ceilings, coarse maximum absolute error below 0.15 C, successive error ratio below 0.6, fine error below 0.04 C, energy balance below 1e-9. This verifies staged conduction time integration against the semi-discrete ODE, not the continuum spatial solution or laser/material physics. Exact-head CI is required before claiming the implementation passes.

## M08 opt-in equilibrium phase enthalpy
The uniform-cell research experiment now accepts explicit solidus/liquidus, latent heat, solid/liquid sensible heat capacity and a source label. No default alloy card is supplied. Enthalpy relative to initial feedstock temperature is conserved; the mushy interval uses linear latent fraction and a constant solid sensible heat capacity, with liquid heat capacity above liquidus. The explicit time-step bound uses the minimum sensible capacity. Outputs expose instantaneous and peak equilibrium liquid fraction separately from the existing beam-marked heuristic consolidation flag. This does not model phase kinetics, irreversible consolidation, liquid flow, powder shrinkage or phase-dependent conductivity. Tests cover invertibility, latent energy, legacy equivalence at zero latent heat and equal capacities, and independently reconstructed cell enthalpy for a synthetic plate-heating problem. The synthetic 40–60 C transition and 20 kJ/kg latent heat are numerical test inputs, not metal material properties. No production UI or default solver behavior is changed. Exact-head CI remains required.

## M08 temperature-dependent effective conductivity
An optional researcher-supplied table explicitly identifies material, source label and synthetic versus reviewed-user-data provenance. It interpolates powder and beam-exposed effective conductivity between ordered temperature knots, rejects extrapolation (including final time-step updates), and uses the maximum table conductivity for conservative stability. Nominal ambient values must agree with the original input. The exposure state remains heuristic and independent of liquid fraction; there is no verified temperature-dependent alloy card or calibrated liquid/powder transport model. Synthetic tests compare constant-table equivalence, variable-conductivity plate heating, energy balance and out-of-range rejection. No production defaults or existing UI behavior change.

## M08 optional gray radiation boundary
Signed Stefan–Boltzmann exchange is applied only to upward-facing cell faces lacking active material immediately above. Explicit emissivity, environment temperature, maximum allowed temperature and source label are required. The maximum flux derivative over the declared temperature range enters the explicit stability ceiling; out-of-range temperatures are rejected rather than clamped. The energy ledger accounts for signed radiative loss/gain separately. Tests cover zero-emissivity equivalence, hot/cold surroundings, cooling, energy balance and range rejection. Direct view to an isothermal environment is an approximation, not a resolved powder bed or chamber model. No shadowing, view factors, optical calibration, or radiation qualification is claimed. Production defaults are unchanged.

## M08 equivalent powder/substrate thermal resistance
An opt-in external resistance boundary replaces side and unsupported downward-face convection with conduction through a half-cell and equivalent powder path to a fixed powder reservoir. The substrate term adds half-cell, contact and substrate resistances in series to the fixed plate reservoir, without double counting the bottom face. The explicit time-step estimate includes a conservative upper bound for additional powder conductance. Separate signed powder exchange is included in the energy balance. A synthetic first-layer one-cell exponential reference, equilibrium and hot-reservoir tests verify the implementation. This is an effective steady resistance approximation, not a resolved powder domain, transient substrate heat capacity, chamber thermocouple prediction or calibrated material model. No production defaults are changed.
