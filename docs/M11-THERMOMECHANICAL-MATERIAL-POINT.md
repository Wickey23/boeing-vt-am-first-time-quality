# M11 print-induced thermomechanical research: initial material-point verification

This is an independent synthetic material-point benchmark, not the completion of M10 or M11. M10 in the master roadmap is structural service-load analysis upgrade; M11 concerns manufacturing residual stress/distortion and depends on M08, M09 and M10. The production structural solver is not changed.

## Model
Small-strain uniaxial total strain = elastic + thermal + plastic strain. Thermal strain is alpha times temperature change. With prescribed total strain, compute a trial stress using Young's modulus and previous plastic strain; clamp to the positive/negative yield stress and update plastic strain by the return increment. With traction-free conditions stress is zero and total strain is thermal plus plastic strain. Start at zero stress and reference temperature. Constant E, yield and expansion coefficient are explicitly supplied with a source label.

## Predeclared synthetic verification
Use E=200000 MPa, yield=200 MPa, alpha=1e-5/K, reference 20 C (illustrative, not an alloy card). A free 300 K temperature rise gives 0.003 strain and zero stress. A fixed 50 K rise gives -100 MPa and returns to zero after cooling. A fixed 300 K rise yields at -200 MPa with -0.002 plastic strain; fixed cooling to reference reverses yield to +200 MPa with -0.001 plastic strain. Idealized traction-free release gives zero axial stress and -0.001 total strain. Check strain decomposition and reject invalid histories or strain above the chosen 5% research bound. Exact-head tests must pass before claiming implementation verification.

## Limitations and next gate
This does not predict a part's residual stress or distortion: a material point has no compatibility/equilibrium with neighbors. Temperature histories are prescribed, not silently taken from command-end snapshots; actual thermal coupling requires deposition stress-free references, adequate temporal sampling, calibrated temperature-dependent mechanical data, and mechanically compatible domain/constraints. Future work must demonstrate structural service solver convergence, geometry-aware mechanical equilibrium and independently measured strain/deflection before validated manufacturing predictions.

## Bonded axial fiber extension
A parallel bundle shares a common axial strain with bending suppressed. Each fiber has explicit area and material data, independently prescribed temperature and history-dependent plastic strain. Fixed total strain returns the required axial reaction. Zero-resultant-force release solves the area-weighted force balance using a monotonic bracketed root; nonunique fully yielded equilibria are withheld. Synthetic two-fiber tests cover differential elastic heating, restrained heating/cooling/release and unequal areas. For two equal 1 mm2 fibers after differential yielding and cooling, the release reference is common strain -0.0005, stresses +100 and -100 MPa, zero resultant force. Thus zero net force can coexist with self-equilibrated internal residual stress. This is not a three-dimensional traction-free equilibrium or distortion result. No mechanical mesh, thermal-history coupling or production behavior changes. Exact-head CI is required.

## Elastic section bending benchmark
Use plane-section compatibility, axial strain at a stiffness-weighted centroid plus curvature times distance from that centroid. Thermal and explicitly prescribed inelastic strains are subtracted from total strain. A free section solves zero axial force and zero bending moment by stiffness-weighted projection; a fixed section returns reactions. Reject singular/ill-conditioned section coordinates, strains exceeding the research bound and trial stresses above yield (nonlinear bending is not implemented). Three equal 1 mm2 fibers at z=-1,0,1 mm with a linear 20,70,120 C temperature gradient have free curvature 0.0005/mm and zero stress, using the synthetic alpha=1e-5/K. A prescribed center-fiber inelastic strain -0.0012 at reference temperature produces self-equilibrated stresses -80,+160,-80 MPa and zero curvature. Translation of the section coordinate origin must preserve curvature and stress. These are idealized elastic section results with prescribed eigenstrains, not a predicted plastic history or full 3D distortion field. Exact-head CI remains required.

## Registered thermal-to-elastic section history
Re-execute the original STL and scan bytes with frame capture. Select a fully occupied contiguous through-height XY grid column; map each physical layer's cell center and area (XY pitch times physical layer thickness) to a section fiber. The user must explicitly acknowledge a stress-free reset at a chosen command-end frame after all selected layers have activated; each fiber uses its own temperature at that frame as reference. Apply fixed zero strain/curvature before the explicit release frame and solve free axial/moment equilibrium thereafter. The output retains part and scan hashes, selection, reference temperatures and command indices. Tests compare transferred temperatures against independently replayed original-byte thermal results and check zero stress at reset, force/moment balance on release and rejection of invalid selection or premature reference. No solidification or actual stress-free state is inferred, and previous plastic history is discarded. Command-end sampling misses intermediate thermal peaks; the model assumes bonded fibers and rejects yielding. It is a one-way elastic section diagnostic, not coupled 3D LPBF mechanics or a validated build-plate removal prediction. Exact-head CI is required.

## Bounded 3D elastic thermal mesh
A separate research solver uses six conforming linear tetrahedra per fully occupied rectangular cell, following the production service solver's discretization pattern without changing that solver. Per-cell isotropic thermal eigenstrain is alpha times temperature minus an explicit reference. Assemble the equivalent thermal nodal forces and stiffness, solve the reduced system with diagonally preconditioned conjugate gradients, reconstruct element stress from elastic strain, and check free-DOF equilibrium, net reaction force and moment. Full-face connectivity is required. The clamped base fixes every base-plane DOF; the alternative 3+2+1 pin constraints remove rigid motion only. A 200-cell cap, small-strain bounds and rejection above supplied von Mises yield stress limit scope. Tests use a two-cell rectangular mesh for exact isotropic free expansion, zero temperature-change solution, clamped thermal stresses, elastic release, differential thermal eigenstrain and failure on disconnected/yielding meshes. The current implementation is an independent 3D benchmark on explicit cells, not yet wired to the registered thermal history; no plasticity, phase-dependent mechanics, conforming CAD mesh, support geometry or validated removal is modeled. Exact-head CI and spatial mechanical convergence remain required.
